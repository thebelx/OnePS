The one piece is PRIM-OK.

by bel  ·  jb.0d01.wtf

Everything in this chain depends on one value. Not a URL knob, not an offset, not a slab size. A function. Its absence is the difference between a chain that runs on every cold boot and one that hits somewhere between 1 in 7 and 1 in 15. Read the section titled Where the road actually ends.

I started this with one question: does pru_bind copy the sockaddr, or retain it? Everything since has been an attempt to answer that. Every attempt goes through the same door. Its name is PRIM-OK.

PRIM-OK is what core.js prints when the SSV primitive lands. It means the fake JSArrayBuffer is live in the heap, p.read8 and p.write8 work, and the whole kernel walk is one function call away. Without it, nothing downstream runs. No BASES. No KBASE-PROBE. No PROC. No PRU. No FN_BYTES.

The entire chain sleeps behind one line of output. That line is the one piece.

Credit where it's due

SOMEONE ELSE found the original SSV shape. The k=2 duplicate-index trick, the predecessor fill, the history.replaceState collision that's NOT my research. Everything below is built on top of it. I'm publishing this because the work deserves a clean writeup, and because the last mile is missing and I want the community to look at it.

What I tried

Every slab, every separator, every predecessor

slab=0x200000 through slab=0xe00000. Only 0x800000 keeps the state blob alive past the drains. Every other value gives Unable to deserialize. That one is solid.

pred=0x80000 gives ZERO-HEADER-MISS. pred=0x100000 gives Unable to deserialize. The window is one page wide. 0xa0000 is the value that has landed twice.

sep, guard, early, bfly — swept every value in range. No combination shifted the failure modes.

Every slot count, every drain size

slots=4000000 fits. slots=8700000 fits with drain:64. slots=9000000 OOMs on a settled boot. Above 8700000 the carrier itself is more than the PS4 13.02 browser's sysmemory pool can hold, and the chain dies at ADDROF-PREP-BEGIN.

The FW 11.00 reference chain runs slots=20000000 — 160 MB. That is 2.2× what 13.02 allows. It is why their chain hits READ-PRIMITIVE-PASS on the first try and mine needs cold boots. Their pattern covers the entire JSC heap. Mine covers a fifth of it.

Timing is a gate, not a knob

Two failure modes bound the correct timing window:

Open too early → the OS is still claiming memory → OOM at ADDROF-PREP-BEGIN. Observed at ~2 min after cold boot on my console.

Open too late → pages have been freed and zeroed by background collection → ZERO-HEADER-MISS hex=0000.... Observed when waiting longer than ~4 minutes.

The working window is narrow. It also moves per console and per boot. The honest thing to do on your own hardware is to sweep intervals — 150 s, 180 s, 210 s, 240 s, 270 s, 300 s — two cold boots per interval, and record which interval produces the fewest OOMs and the fewest ZERO-HEADER-MISSes. That interval is your window.

The four outcomes

Every cold boot ends in exactly one of four classifications. Everything else in the log is commentary.

tagmeaning
OOMdied before SSV. Memory budget or timing.
DESERIALIZE-FAILSSV ran, state blob clobbered. Unable to deserialize.
PLACEMENT-FAILSSV ran, blob survived, cell landed wrong. ZERO-HEADER-MISS / VALIDATION-MISMATCH.
PRIM-OKSSV ran, cell landed on pattern. Primitive live.

The URL knobs change how often you get each tag. They do not change the fact that only PRIM-OK proceeds.

Where the road actually ends

Here is the thing I have been circling. There is one function in core.js whose absence is the difference between a per-boot hit rate between 7% and 15% and a per-boot hit rate near 100%:

beforeCriticalLoad

core.js accepts an option called beforeCriticalLoad. If you pass one, it runs between the SSV grooming and the state-blob deserialization. Its job is to nudge JSC's allocator into committing a fresh cell at exactly the fake address. With it, placement is deterministic. Without it, you get a stub that writes a string to a hidden div and creates a Blob, and does nothing else.

That stub is what I have. That stub is why I cold boot.

The reference chain on FW 11.00 does not have a real beforeCriticalLoad either. It wins because 160 MB of pattern covers the entire heap — any cell the allocator hands out is inside the pattern. On 13.02, no carrier is big enough. The missing callback is what substitutes for the missing memory.

Whoever has a real beforeCriticalLoad for 13.02 is holding the one piece. Everything in this chain depends on that one function. Every other knob — slab, pred, sep, slots, drain — is a workaround for its absence.

The probability model

Two independent gates. The chain must survive the first to test the second.

P(success) = P(T) × P(A | T)

T is the timing/allocator gate: the boot lands in the window where the OS has stopped claiming memory but GC hasn't zeroed the pages. A is the placement gate: the deserializer hands out the cell at exactly the address the predecessor pattern covers.

Observed on my console:

quantityestimatenote
P(T)~0.25fraction of cold boots in the timing window
P(A | T)~0.15fraction of in-window boots where placement lands
P(success)~0.041 in ~25 cold boots

The exact number is uncertain. Two PRIM-OK events were observed across the test session. Depending on how you count the trials — all cold boots, or only the ones that reached the SSV stage — the point estimate is between 7% and 13%. Two successes is too small a sample to distinguish 7% from 15%. The writeup's "13–15%" figure should be read as a working estimate with a wide confidence interval, not a measured rate.

The URL knobs affect two things and only two things: whether the carrier covers the leaked string (coverage floor), and whether the total allocation fits under the browser's sysmemory pool (memory ceiling). Whether they affect P(A | T) at all — placement probability given a good timing roll — is an open question the repository can answer but which has not been tested in a controlled way.

Formula sheet

Two exact formulas, and everything else empirical.

Coverage floor. The carrier must be large enough to cover the leaked string. If copiedLength chars were leaked:

slots ≥ (copiedLength − 8) / 4

On 13.02 the leaked string is either ~924,176 chars (short roll) or ~34,478,608 chars (long roll). So the floor is either ~231,042 slots or ~8,619,650 slots.

Memory ceiling. The total allocation must fit under the browser's sysmemory pool. On fn_leak.html after cold boot, observed ceiling is ~84 MB ±2 MB. The budget is:

slots × 8 + drain × drainsz + slab + pred + holes ≤ 84 MB

Which gives slots ≤ ~9,175,040 with the working values of the other knobs.

Everything else is empirical. The working slab and pred values depend on the boot's KASLR seed and JSC's allocator state, neither of which is observable from JS before the SSV runs. slab=0x800000 and pred=0xa0000 are landings on a per-boot curve whose shape is not visible from JavaScript. They are not derived.

How to run

Cold boot required. Power off the PS4 and unplug it for 30 seconds. Plug back in, power on. Open the browser in the timing window you found by sweeping. Do not open any other tab. A warm boot or a reload reuses the previous renderer's heap and the chain will OOM at ADDROF-PREP-BEGIN.
If you see AUTO-RETRY-SCHEDULED in the log, you are running a stale cached copy of core.js or fn_leak.js. The current build has maxAttempts: 1 and will not retry in place. Serve the folder with Cache-Control: no-store, or hard-clear the browser cache between runs. The retry loop does not help — the second attempt reuses the first attempt's heap garbage and fails at a different stage.

The launcher below applies the working defaults automatically. Press the button, the chain runs once, one cold boot, one roll. If PRIM-OK does not print, cold boot and try again.

Portability


  

    Everything published here is 13.02. The missing piece — beforeCriticalLoad
    — is likely portable to the rest of the 13.x line, because it operates on JSC's
    allocator, not on the kernel. The JSC deduplication bug that lets a
    history.state clone alias a freed cell is a JavaScriptCore bug, not a
    Sony bug. If the same shape of history.replaceState call produces the
    same deserialization path on 13.04 as it does on 13.02, the callback should work
    unchanged, and so should the JS-side heap shaping in core.js.
  


  

    What definitely does not travel is every offset in ps4_offsets.js. The
    kernel RVAs, the libkernel stub table, the WebKit gadget offsets, and every anchor
    (k__error, wk_expm1_builtin, k_kl_lock,
    k_sysent_661, allproc) are compiled into a specific
    firmware build. On 13.04 they are all different. Anyone porting has to re-derive
    every one from their own kernel dump — the same procedure documented for 13.02,
    with the same tools.
  


  

    For 13.50 and 13.52 I have nothing. I don't know if the callback ports, I don't
    know if the SSV bug is present in their WebKit build, I don't know if the offsets
    are anywhere near 13.04's. Those are hypotheses worth testing, not results.
  


  

    Disclaimer. I have personally tested this chain on 13.02 and only 13.02.
    Everything above about 13.04, 13.50, and 13.52 is reasoning, not empirical result.
    Do not take it as a guarantee — take it as a hypothesis worth testing. If you get
    any non-13.02 result, whether that's a working PRIM-OK, a new failure
    mode, or an offset that differs, please reach out.
  


  

    Everything in this chain depends on one value. On 13.02, that value is
    beforeCriticalLoad. On every other firmware, it's that plus every
    offset in ps4_offsets.js. The first one is portable in theory. The
    second one is a week of Ghidra per firmware.
